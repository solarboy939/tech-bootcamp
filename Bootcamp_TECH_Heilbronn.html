Today’s focus — the three highlighted vehicles (4, 5, 6). They’re the ones corporates get wrong most often, because they all look like “startup collaboration” but answer completely different questions.
Vehicle 04 · Lowest commitment, fastest learning

Venture
Clienting

First pilot in 6–12 weeks

When the problem is generic and a startup somewhere already solved it — you don’t need to build, you need to buy a pilot. Curate, pilot, scale.

The trap: Treating Venture Clienting like procurement. The point isn’t the cheap pilot. It’s the learning velocity and the optionality on a second contract.
Right when
Problem type
Generic · non‑proprietary
Solution maturity
Exists in market
Timeline
Need it this year
Equity appetite
None to low
Example: AI assistant for invoice processing in finance. 50 startups exist. You don’t build — you run a 12-week pilot with two and pick the winner.
Vehicle 05 · Curated pipeline around a theme

Accelerator
Track

10–20 startups in your topic per batch

When you have multiple related problems in one domain and want a steady pipeline of options — not a one-off pilot. You shape selection criteria, you sit on the demo day jury, you get first-mover access.

Real example · Campus Founders: ~300 applications per batch → corporate partners flag 10–20 they want to engage with → startups arrive at demo day with two pilots already pencilled in.
Right when
Problem type
Theme · not single use-case
What you bring
Domain expertise + customer access
Timeline
3–6 months per cohort
Equity appetite
Optional · deal-flow access first
When it beats Venture Clienting: You don’t know exactly which problem matters most yet. A track lets you see 20 framings of it before committing capital to one.
Vehicle 06 · Build what doesn’t exist yet

Venture
Building

10 ventures in 3 years · €10M studio budget (proof point: Italy)

When the problem is proprietary — or so specific that no startup is solving it yet — you team up with a founder, contribute your data and pilot, and build the company together. Founder-first: 70–80% equity to the founding team, 20–30% to corporate partners.

Example: AI for port logistics, built with PSA (Italy’s largest port operator). They contributed 12 months of yard-movement data no normal startup could access — that data was the unfair advantage.
Right when
Problem type
Proprietary · uniquely yours
Solution maturity
Nobody built it yet
Your contribution
Data · pilot · first revenue
Outcome
Equity + exit + pilot customer
Pooled studio idea: 4–5 anchor corporates × €1–2M each = €10M PropTech / Mittelstand studio anchored on Campus Founders & LilaLu. Coalition of the willing.
Part 2 · Problem Framing

Anatomy of a workable problem

Before you pick a vehicle, write the problem in a way that lets you pick the right one

✓ A workable problem

“Our procurement team spends 30% of their week reconciling invoices across 14 ERP systems. Anyone in mid-size manufacturing has this problem.”

  • Specific user / role affected
  • Frequency & magnitude quantified
  • Distinguishes “ours alone” vs “industry-generic”
  • Implies the unit of value (time, cost, risk)
  • Doesn’t prescribe the solution
× Not yet workable

“We need AI in finance.”

  • No user, no scenario, no metric
  • Mixes the wish with the technology
  • Can’t tell if anyone else has it
  • Forces a Venture Clienting pilot of “anything AI”
  • Picks the vehicle before the problem is understood
The framing test: Read your problem statement out loud. If a peer corporate in a different industry could nod and say “same here” — it’s generic (probably Venture Clienting). If they look puzzled — it’s probably proprietary (Venture Building territory).
Exercise 1 · 8 min

Sketch your problem

How to play

One problem you actually have. Right now.

Pick the one innovation problem in your org you keep coming back to. Fill out the canvas. Hit Add to the wall. Five minutes.

  1. Be specific about who feels it
  2. Quantify the pain if you can
  3. Don’t prescribe the solution
  4. Note what you’ve already tried
Tip: If you can’t describe it in one sentence, you’re still describing a wish, not a problem.

Wrap-up · 10 min

Three takeaways. Three questions.

What you walk out with — and what we’d like the room to argue about

01

The vehicle is downstream of the problem. Reframe before you reach for a startup.

02

Generic problems don’t deserve Venture Building. Proprietary ones don’t deserve a procurement RFP. Match it.

03

Found a peer in the room with the same problem? Pool the budget — a coalition of the willing beats a solo studio every time.
